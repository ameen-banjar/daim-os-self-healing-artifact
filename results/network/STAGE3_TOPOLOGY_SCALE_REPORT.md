# Multiple Topologies, Scale, and Overhead Report

Date: 15 August 2026
Environment: Ubuntu 24.04 LTS ARM64 (Multipass VM `daim-lab`), Open vSwitch 3.3.4, Mininet 2.3.0 --
exact version match to every prior live experiment in this evidence set.
Evidence level: `measured_emulation_topology_scale` (live-network parts), plus a pure-Python,
no-network-needed BFS microbenchmark.

## What this closes

Section 10's remaining "multiple topologies + scale + overhead" evidence-gate item. Every live
experiment run so far in this evidence set (single-host, multi-OVS, hold-down flapping,
asymmetric-interface, hold-down sensitivity) used one of two structurally IDENTICAL four-switch
diamond graphs. This round runs the real, unmodified `daim_link_agent.py` -- via a new generic
topology generator (`topology_gen.py`), not a hand-built diamond -- against three structurally
different topologies, one of them exceeding 200 switches (in a pure-logic microbenchmark) and one
exceeding 10 switches live (closing the explicit scale target), while also measuring the agent
process's own CPU/memory footprint and deriving a flow-mod message-overhead metric.

## Part 1: BFS scaling microbenchmark (`bfs_scaling_benchmark.py`)

Section 2.5 states plainly: "BFS runtime on a 200-node graph is under 0.3 ms on contemporary
hardware... the 200-node bound above is a design target, not a measured result." This closes that
gap directly: the real, unmodified `bfs_path()` function was timed (2000 repetitions per size, this
Mac, no Mininet/OVS needed since the function is pure in-process graph search with zero I/O) against
a deterministic grid-graph generator at sizes from 9 to 2025 nodes, with the source/dest hosts
attached at opposite grid corners -- the longest possible shortest path for a given node count, the
BFS worst case for this generator, not a favourable one.

| Nodes | Edges | Path hops | Mean | Max | Repetitions |
|------:|------:|----------:|-----:|----:|------------:|
| 9     | 11    | 4         | 0.004 ms | 0.012 ms | 2000 |
| 25    | 39    | 8         | 0.013 ms | 0.022 ms | 2000 |
| 49    | 83    | 12        | 0.028 ms | 0.036 ms | 2000 |
| 100   | 179   | 18        | 0.063 ms | 0.092 ms | 2000 |
| **196** | **363** | **26**  | **0.133 ms** | **0.205 ms** | **2000** |
| 400   | 759   | 38        | 0.298 ms | 0.586 ms | 2000 |
| 1024  | 1983  | 62        | 0.817 ms | 11.4 ms (outlier) | 2000 |
| 2025  | 3959  | 88        | 1.84 ms  | 63.0 ms (outlier) | 2000 |

**The design target holds, measured, at the point closest to 200 nodes**: 196 nodes, mean 0.133 ms,
max 0.205 ms across 2000 repetitions -- both comfortably under the cited 0.3 ms bound. Honest
finding, not hidden: the bound does NOT hold cleanly by 400 nodes (max 0.586 ms) and the two largest
sizes show occasional multi-millisecond outliers (11.4 ms, 63.0 ms) attributed to this being a
shared, non-realtime laptop host (Python GC pauses / OS scheduling jitter), not algorithmic
super-linearity -- the mean/median at every size still scales smoothly and consistently with node
count. Section 2.5 is updated to report this measured data directly rather than continuing to cite
an unmeasured design target.

## Part 2: Multi-topology correctness and scale (`stage3_topology_scale.py`)

### Topologies tested

- **`linear_10`**: a 10-switch chain with NO redundant path for any edge -- deliberately the
  topology self-healing cannot succeed on. Tests that the agent reports a genuinely unrecoverable
  failure honestly (`repair_failed`, no false-positive success, no crash), rather than only ever
  being exercised against topologies where a repair happens to be possible.
- **`ring_8`** and **`ring_20`**: an 8-switch and a 20-switch cycle, single-fault-tolerant
  everywhere, run at two sizes on the identical topology shape to see the repair-action-time trend
  against a growing hop count in isolation from topology type.
- **`fattree_k4`**: a 20-switch (exceeding the >10-switch scale target), three-tier fat-tree with
  genuine multi-path redundancy at both the aggregation and core layers. The failed edge
  (`a0_0-c0_0`) was deliberately chosen as the second hop of the primary path, not the trivial
  host-adjacent edge, so the discovered reroute is forced through a DIFFERENT core switch
  (`c0_1`) -- confirmed directly from the agent's own log (`repair_installed` path
  `[e0_0, a0_0, c0_1, a3_0, e3_0]` vs. the original `[e0_0, a0_0, c0_0, a3_0, e3_0]`), not a
  same-pod or trivially local detour.

All four topologies were generated by a new shared `topology_gen.py`, with port numbers computed to
match Mininet's own per-node incrementing assignment exactly (verified by loading each generated
config into the real `bfs_path()`/topology-consistency checks before any live run).

### Results

| Topology     | Switches | Links | Monitored ifaces | Reps | Path hops | Repair succeeded | Mean repair-action time | Mean packet loss |
|--------------|---------:|------:|------------------:|-----:|----------:|:-----------------:|-------------------------:|-------------------:|
| linear_10    | 10       | 9     | 18                 | 1    | 9         | No (by design)     | -- (`repair_failed`)     | 88.3%             |
| ring_8       | 8        | 8     | 16                 | 3    | 4 -> 4    | Yes, 3/3            | 294.9 ms                 | 16.1%             |
| ring_20      | 20       | 20    | 40                 | 3    | 10 -> 10  | Yes, 3/3            | 714.4 ms                 | 38.3%             |
| fattree_k4   | 20       | 32    | 64                 | 3    | 4 -> 4    | Yes, 3/3            | 267.8 ms                 | 13.3%             |

**10/10 repetitions matched their designed outcome exactly** (`recoverable_by_design` vs.
`repair_succeeded`): the unrecoverable `linear_10` case correctly and safely produced
`repair_failed` (no crash, no false-positive success, current_path left `None` per Section 5.2's
existing failure-honesty contract) rather than either hanging or wrongly claiming success; every
`ring_8`/`ring_20`/`fattree_k4` fault was correctly detected and repaired.

**Repair-action time scales with hop count, not topology type**: `ring_8` (4 hops) and `fattree_k4`
(4 hops, but crossing a completely different switch role hierarchy -- edge/agg/core, not a flat
cycle) produced near-identical repair times (294.9 ms vs. 267.8 ms), while `ring_20` (10 hops, same
topology *type* as `ring_8`) took roughly 2.4x longer (714.4 ms) -- consistent with `path_to_flows()`
installing 2 flow-mod calls per hop, so a 2.5x longer path does roughly 2.5x the real OVS I/O work.
This is the first live evidence in this evidence set that repair time is governed by path length,
not by which topology shape the path happens to live in -- previously only ever measured on paths of
length 2-3 in the diamond and multi-OVS topologies.

**Packet loss scales with hop count for the same underlying reason** (more flow-mod calls before the
new path is fully live): 16.1% (`ring_8`, 4 hops) -> 38.3% (`ring_20`, 10 hops), while `fattree_k4`
(also 4 hops, different topology shape) matches `ring_8`'s magnitude (13.3%) rather than tracking
switch count (both are 20-switch topologies, but `fattree_k4`'s *path* is the same length as
`ring_8`'s) -- confirming path length, not raw switch count, is the load-bearing scale variable for
both repair time and loss, not topology size per se.

### Overhead: agent CPU and memory footprint

Sampled directly from `/proc/<agent_pid>/status` (VmRSS) and `/proc/<agent_pid>/stat`
(utime+stime), immediately after `agent_started` and again immediately after the repair (or
`repair_failed`) decision:

| Topology   | Monitored ifaces | Mean RSS delta | Mean CPU time consumed |
|------------|------------------:|----------------:|-------------------------:|
| linear_10  | 18                 | 4 KB             | 0.010 s                  |
| ring_8     | 16                 | 8 KB             | 0.013 s                  |
| ring_20    | 40                 | 14.7 KB          | 0.030 s                  |
| fattree_k4 | 64                 | 9.3 KB           | 0.010 s                  |

The agent's memory footprint is negligible and stable regardless of monitored-interface count (4-15
KB delta across the whole fault-detection-to-repair window, against a ~16.9 MB baseline RSS) --
consistent with an event-driven design holding no per-interface buffers that grow with topology
size. CPU time consumed during the repair window is small in absolute terms (10-30 ms of real CPU
time) and tracks path length (`ring_20`'s 10-hop repair consumed roughly 2-3x `ring_8`'s 4-hop
repair's CPU time) rather than the number of monitored interfaces (`fattree_k4` monitors 4x as many
interfaces as `ring_8` but consumed the same CPU time, since both have 4-hop repair paths) -- the
same path-length-governs-cost pattern as repair-action time and packet loss above, now confirmed
across three independent metrics.

### Message overhead

Derived analytically from `path_to_flows()`'s own documented contract (2 OpenFlow flow-mod calls
per traversed hop: one per direction), applied to the real hop counts each repetition's log actually
reported -- not re-measured by a separate counting mechanism, since the contract itself is simple
and already directly verifiable by code reading. `linear_10`'s failed repair still issues 2 x 9 = 18
withdraw-only messages (the old path is torn down even though no new path is staged); `ring_8` and
`fattree_k4` (4 hops each) issue 16 total (8 withdraw + 8 install); `ring_20` (10 hops) issues 40 (20
withdraw + 20 install) -- linear in hop count, as `path_to_flows()`'s per-hop loop guarantees by
construction.

### What this does and does not establish

Establishes: the agent's BFS-based routing genuinely generalises across three structurally different
topology shapes (chain, cycle, tree-of-trees), not just the one diamond shape exercised until now;
correct, safe handling of a genuinely unrecoverable topology; a live confirmation the fat-tree
reroute crosses a different core switch, not a trivial local detour; a real, live >10-switch (20)
deployment; and that repair time, packet loss, CPU time, and flow-mod message count all scale
primarily with REPAIR PATH LENGTH, not topology size or monitored-interface count. Does not
establish: a statistically replicated dataset at each topology (n=1 for the necessarily-single-outcome
`linear_10` case, n=3 for the others -- a preliminary exploration, consistent with this evidence
set's existing convention of deferring the final replicated dataset to Layer 2's later step); formal
baseline comparison (fast-failover / controller-driven recovery on these same topologies, Section
10's separate, still-open item); or topologies substantially larger than 20 switches (a natural
future extension, not attempted here since 20 already exceeds the explicit >10 target).
